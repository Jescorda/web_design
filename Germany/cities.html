Major Cities

From Historic Capitals to Modern Metropolises

1. Berlin

The capital and largest city, Berlin is a global center of culture, politics, media, and science. Known for its reminders of a turbulent history, it is now famous for its nightlife, diverse architecture, and contemporary arts scene. Reminders of the city's turbulent 20th-century history include the Holocaust Memorial and the Berlin Wall graffiti remains.

  • Landmark: Brandenburg Gate
  • Known For: Berlin Wall, Museums Island, Techno Clubs

2. Munich (München)

The capital of Bavaria, Munich is known for its centuries-old architecture mixed with modern technology hubs (like BMW). It is the home of Oktoberfest and famously welcoming beer halls. Munich is built on the banks of the River Isar north of the Bavarian Alps.

"Munich is where traditional Lederhosen meet modern innovation."

3. Hamburg

Germany's "Gateway to the World," Hamburg is a major port city on the Elbe River. It has more bridges than Venice and Amsterdam combined and features the stunning Elbphilharmonie concert hall. It is famous for its hundreds of canals, parkland areas, and the historic Speicherstadt (Warehouse District).

4. Cologne (Köln)

A 2,000-year-old city spanning the Rhine River, famous for its massive Gothic Cathedral (Kölner Dom), which dramatically survived WWII bombings. It is a major cultural hub for the Rhineland, hosting annual Carnival celebrations.
